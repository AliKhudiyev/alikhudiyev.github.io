Let me tell you a funny, yet true, story. My schooling years started off
in some school that did not offer almost anything good to its students:
whether it be the level of education, or the maturity or noblety of
people went there, or lawfulness of the administrative and educational
processes that took place. This was before I changed this school and
decided to go to vastly and imcomparably better one, which was TDV-BTL.
As I already said, most of the students who went to this joke of a
school didn't even seem to be there for getting education. Many of them
were immature and poorly educated. At that school, I was considered to
be one of the smartest students. In certain sense, it was hard to be
smart and devote yourself to your education in such a badly managed
environment, but in another sense, it was easy to be the best among the
worst, frankly speaking. Going back to the vastly better school, which
was TDV, I became a student there after passing their internal
examination. At the time, TDV had a "double-class" structure, which
meant that each class were divided into 2 (sub-)classes; for example,
instead of having a single $9^\text{th}$ grade with Group-1
specialization and a single $10^\text{th}$ grade with Group-1
specialization, there were two $9^\text{th}$ grades (9A and 9F) and two
$10^\text{th}$ grades (10A and 10F). The A classes had students with
higher entrance scores than those of students of the F classes. Based on
my score, I was put as the first highest-scoring student in the F class.
Once again, I was the best of the worst... *Obviously, I use the word
"worst" in a certain context here, not trying to demean anyone in any
bad way; not to mention that grades mean nothing in the big picture of
how things evolve.*

After I graduated from the school, I applied to study computer science
at UFAZ, a double-degree university. They had an internal examination
for the selection process, and long story short: I had the highest
score, which was literally one less than the score that would allow me
to study wihout paying annual university fees. I went to study the
foundation year in another field, which was free of annual fees and then
got transferred to the CS faculty at UFAZ with an exemption from annual
university fees due to my good performance on the foundation year, which
was appreciated by a
<a target="_blank" href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/charlotte-payen-724160100/">person</a> in
charge at that time. Anyway, once again, I got the best consequence of
the worst situation! There were other times similar things happened to
me after I graduated from UFAZ with a computer science degree, but I
won't bother going through each one of them. The point is not about my
stories alone: the point is actually something bigger than any of this
in some sense. So, what IS the point?!

The point is: you don't actually want to stay as *"the best of the
worst"* longer than needed; you want to be the best of the worst so that
you can transition to being *"the worst of the best"* later on. Progress
in life doesn't come from being at where you are for your entire life.
It comes from moving from being the best among the worst to being the
worst among the best, over and over again.

It is like the drawing above: the worst of the worst $\rightarrow$ the
best of the worst $\rightarrow$ the worst of the best $\rightarrow$ the
best of the best. One thing is that the progress is probably felt the
strongest when you make a transitioning from being *the worst of the
best* to being *the worst of the best*. While it is tempting to even
think about being *the best of the best*, you may find life more
intriguing when you decide to be the worst of the best at a different
area outside your expertise. It is often harder to go as below as the
worst of the worst once you are playing in the league of the best
players, but going to being the best of the worst is somewhat easier
even though you are now in the league of the worst.

The life will hit you with many ironies from time to time. All you
should do at those moments is to smile at the irony and work your way
through it somehow. You will know it is time to move one step up when it
feels like you are the best of the worst. You do not want to stay there
for a long time. The right place for most of the people is the place
where they are considered the worst of the best. Even being the best of
the best may not be as sustainable as being at one of the two steps
before it. If you reach to the point where you are considered to be the
best of the best, then you should probably not be too afraid to jump to
another stairs, you know, for the fun of it.
