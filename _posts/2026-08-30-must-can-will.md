title: "I must, I can, I will"

Three phases of overcoming something hard almost always starts with you
thinking that *you must do it*, then thinking maybe *you can actually do
it* as you keep spending more time on it and developing better
understanding or skills you didn't have before, then finally thinking to
yourself that *you will obviously do it* and it's just a matter of time
now. If you are obsessed with this thing, whatever it might be, then by
definition, you naturally start from the first phase by feeling like you
must do it, no matter what. It is worth mentioning that it might very
well be the case that you are obsessed with that thing: it is just that
you MUST do it; it can be studying a hard subject that must pass because
otherwise you will fail from the semester, or it can be exercising daily
to avoid atrophy in your muscles because otherwise your health will
degrade significantly, or something completely different. Believe it or
not, transitioning from the "I can" phase to "I will" phase is easier
than going from "I must" to "I can". The reason is intuitively simply:
once you see or prove to yourself that you can do something that you
couldn't before, you will keep doing it more; and this process may go on
until you master the underlying thing. Once the can hits, the will
follows. However, most people struggle the most when they are in the
very beginning of this potentially long and time-taking process -- when
they feel like they can't but they are burdened by the pressure of the
must. That's where stoicism type of mentality may actually work: turn
off the feelings and do what must be done regardless. You will feel the
kick of dopamine flooding your system the moment you start switching
from "I must" to "I can". That's also where you will get the most
exciting Aha! moments of your life. The "will" part will follow
naturally once you feel like you have actually cracked the shell.
