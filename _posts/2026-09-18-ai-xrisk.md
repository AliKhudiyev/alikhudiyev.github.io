If you follow AI news, you will quickly realize that the life nowadays
has become a never-ending back and forth tension between two trends:

<figure>
<img style="margin: auto;" src="/assets/blog/agi-xrisk-trend-meme-transparent.png" width="40%"/>
</figure>

Every once in a while (maybe $\approx$6 months or so)
hunanity achieves AGI for the first time... one more time... At this
point I have lost count of how many times AGI has been achieved either
internally or externally. The AI chatbots that you use today are
apparently the same as a PhD-level human being, if not better. Big
companies state things like this and then drop the mic on the stage
before going to get more funding and investment for their next AGI
reveal. What is interesting is that from the statements they make it
seems like apparently they have figured out how to measure intelligence.
Can you guess how? Standartized tests and benchmarks, you say? Oh, I
hope not, because if these ways of measuring intelligence was successful
academic institutions wouldn't lose their credibility over these bulshit
knowledge assessment strategies in the first place. I wonder whether the
smell of sarcasm in the air is stronger or the stupidity of the AGI
claims being made based on standartized tests. Answering multiple-choice
quizzes has never been this important for the AI companies, frankly
speaking.

<span style="font-size:20px;">No, AGI is not here.</span> It has never been. But if you told this to x-risk
guys out there, they would be very upset; they wouldn't be able to write
sci-fi stories over this stuff anymore.

<figure>
<img style="margin: auto;" src="/assets/blog/agi-xrisk-meme.png" width="40%" />
</figure>

The "AGI is here" trend is often followed by "AGI will kill us" trend.
X-risk -- the existensial risk... There are certain people that just
spawn out of nowhere and then starts talking about the x-risk sci-fi
nonstop. They often push their own versions of the x-risk narratives
without scientifically backed up justifications. The arguments typically
go like "losing control is bad, too smart means too dangerous, there is
no reason to keep dumber beings around" and other similar very
vibe-justified statements.

<span style="font-size:20px;">No, x-risk is not here.</span> But if you told this to the x-risk folks out
there, they would be very upset; they get upset all the time; they *ARE*
upset all the time...
